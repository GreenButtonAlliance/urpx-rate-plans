## What a service arrangement is

A service arrangement is a fully-determined selection over the plan space: one `urpx:RatePlanVersion`, plus zero or more `urpx:RatePlanModifierVersion`s applicable to it, plus one `urpx:ProfileAlternative` per dimension either of them leaves open. Determined means nothing remains to choose: it resolves to prices for any instant in its window with no further input. It is the thing a customer takes service under, and it is the unit an OpenADR 3 price server publishes as a program for an EMS to subscribe to.

URPX v0.5.1 has no term for it. Every `owl:ObjectProperty` whose range is one of the four plan classes was enumerated: there are fifteen and none has a composition domain. `urpx:hasModifierOptions` states availability, `urpx:applicableToRatePlan` states compatibility, and `urpx:AccountServiceAgreement` is a stub whose own `skos:note` pushes account data out of scope. A `urpx:RatePlanVersion` is the base plan's snapshot, not the composition.

An arrangement is identified by its own components: the base version IRI, the sorted modifier version IRIs, and the sorted alternative selections. A MIDAS RIN is an optional external attestation, attached only where it is confidently derivable. It is never the identity, and this report neither reads nor assigns one.

## What this report counts

**Enumerated at the grain of one base version plus at most one modifier version.** That grain is what the corpus can defend. Taking every subset of each base's applicable modifiers instead would give 768 arrangements, and most of those combinations are not real: CARE and FERA are mutually exclusive by their own source text, and URPX v0.5.1 has no vocabulary for stating exclusivity between two `urpx:RatePlanModifier`s, so nothing in the corpus can rule the combination out. Enumerating combinations the tariffs forbid would be worse than under-enumerating. What a fuller enumeration would need is set out below.

**Superseded versions are excluded.** A superseded version is not a service arrangement: nobody takes service under it. Supersession is derived from the data, never from directory names, because a directory count is not a version count. Two filing directories can hold one version node: CPAU E-1 is filed at 2025-07-01 and again at 2025-09-01, and both carry `cpau:e-1-2025-07-01`, because the base rates did not change and only the modifiers operative at the snapshot did.

**Open dimensions are reported as an expansion factor, not multiplied into the enumerated set.** Each enumerated arrangement stands for the product of its open dimensions' alternative counts; both numbers are below.

## What a fuller enumeration would need

MIDAS publishes 18 rate identifiers for PG&E E-TOU-D, and their `alt_name` values name the overlays: CARE, CCA, FERA, Medical Baseline, Smart Rate. A rate identifier there names the composition, not the plan, so those 18 are that one plan's arrangements as the utility itself publishes them.

Up to 3 overlays stack at once, so the at-most-one grain here under-enumerates by a long way.

And 2 pairs never co-occur across those 18: CARE with FERA; CCA with Smart Rate. CARE with FERA is exclusive by the tariffs' own text; any others may be exclusive or may be a MIDAS gap, and the list does not distinguish them. Either way it is the shape of constraint the corpus has no way to state, and it is why enumerating full subsets here would manufacture arrangements no customer can take.

Three things would close the gap. A way to say two modifiers are mutually exclusive, which URPX v0.5.1 does not have. A baseline that composes a base with more than one modifier at a time, which the present one does not do. And a selection recorded per open dimension, so that a rate can be attributed to the alternative it belongs to rather than to the arrangement as a whole.

The corpus already models the same exclusivity both ways. PG&E states income tier as a `urpx:ProfileAlternatives` dimension whose alternatives are mutually exclusive by construction. Elsewhere CARE and FERA are separate `urpx:RatePlanModifier`s with nothing to say they cannot both apply, and 13 base versions here accept both of them: `sce:d-2026-03-20`, `sce:tou-d-4-9pm-2026-03-20`, `sce:tou-d-5-8pm-2026-03-20`, `sce:tou-d-prime-2026-03-20`, `sdge:dr-2026-04-01`, `sdge:dr-ses-2026-04-01`, `sdge:ev-tou-2-2026-04-01`, `sdge:ev-tou-2026-04-01`, `sdge:ev-tou-5-2026-04-01`, `sdge:tou-dr-2026-04-01`, `sdge:tou-dr1-2026-04-01`, `sdge:tou-dr2-2026-04-01`, `sdge:tou-elec-2026-04-01`. One shape is machine-checkable and the other is not.

## Provenance

Servability is read from `dev/baseline/marginal-rates.edn`, not re-resolved here. The figures are as of that baseline: 19 probe instants per key, resolved by clj-urpx 0.30.0. An arrangement counts as servable only when every one of its baseline rows resolved at every one of those instants. Re-record the baseline deliberately (`clojure -M:validate --record-baseline`) and this report moves with it.

Every baseline row is accounted for: each is either reached by an arrangement here or belongs to a superseded version. A row that is neither makes this generator refuse rather than report.

`reports/ready-for-use.md` rolls these outcomes up to one row per filing directory. `[modeling].complete` in each `tariff.toml` answers a different question, whether the JSON-LD fully captures its source PDF, and is not used here.
