Auto-generated by `dev/list_rate_identifiers.py`. Do not edit by hand.

## An identifier is an attestation, not an identity

A CEC MIDAS Rate Identification Number is an optional external attestation about a service arrangement. It is never the arrangement's identity. An arrangement is identified intrinsically, by its base version IRI, its sorted modifier version IRIs and its sorted alternative selections, and that is what [`reports/service-arrangements.md`](./service-arrangements.md) enumerates.

**No identifier is written into any filing.** Not one `urpx:RatePlanIdentifier` and not one `urpx:RatePlanModifierIdentifier` in this corpus carries a RIN, and the generator refuses to produce this report if one ever does. Every identifier below is cited in the free-text `urpx:name` of a `urpx:Price`, as a note about where that number came from.

Two reasons, both checkable against the artifacts:

- Every identifier the corpus cites names a **composition**: base plan, plus the export tariff, plus a vintage, plus a supply arrangement. Hanging one on the modifier node would assert that the modifier alone is what California means by the code.
- `urpx-shacl:RatePlanModifierIdentifierShape` is `sh:closed true` over exactly `skos:notation`, `urpx:scheme` and `urpx:identifierJurisdiction` (URPX v0.5.1), so there is no slot in which to qualify the scope of what the notation names.

Their 5 mirror-segment siblings, `USCA-SDXX-NB00-0000`, `USCA-SDXX-NB23-0000`, `USCA-SDXX-NB24-0000`, `USCA-SDXX-NB25-0000`, `USCA-SDXX-NB26-0000`, are absent too: 5 of 5. **The absence is about the rate code, not about the segment.** The rate codes the corpus asserts that the snapshot does not hold are `NB00`, `NB23`, `NB24`, `NB25`, `NB26`; the ones it does hold are `0602`, `0603`, `0604`, `0605`, `0606`, `3500`, `3600`, `3900`, `4000`. Singling out the `XX`-first five treats a property of the segment as the explanation for an absence that falls on both segments equally.

The pair carries a distinction, and the corpus uses it consistently: the `XX`-first identifiers are cited only on `sdge:nbt-pd-export-rate-generation` and their siblings only on `sdge:nbt-pd-export-rate-delivery`. A transposition would not divide along the charge like that.

On the segment itself: `XX` occupies the second half of 1,741 snapshot identifiers and the first half of none. Read against the structure above, a first-half `XX` means *this utility supplies the energy, someone else delivers it*, the exact mirror of the delivery-only code and the way a generation-only series would have to be written. Coherent under the grammar; unattested in this snapshot.

The strings were not composed here. `scripts/extract-sdge-nbt-eec-values.py` reads them out of the `RIN` column of the utility's own MIDAS-format upload files and refuses if the column is not constant per vintage and component.

**Verdict: not a transposition, and nothing to fix in `us/`.** Confidence is high on the reading of the segment: the two halves divide along the two charges, and the strings come verbatim from the utility's own upload files. Confidence is lower on whether MIDAS publishes this rate-code family at all, which rests on a single snapshot taken through a single enumeration path and not refreshable at present. If a refreshed snapshot still lacks the family, the change worth making is to record the snapshot's own identifiers for the same arrangement alongside these, not to alter these.

## How to read a row

An arrangement is the unit. The identifier is a note attached beside it saying *California publishes this composition under this code*. A consumer verifying prices should resolve the arrangement, then look the identifier up; a consumer building an identity should use the arrangement's own components and never the identifier.
